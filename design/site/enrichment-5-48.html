What does Qur’an 5:48 actually confirm?

Two words in one verse, muṣaddiq and muhaymin, settle most of the Islamic Dilemma. Here is what each means.

Editorial team · 7 min read · Draft for review

Advocates of the Islamic Dilemma read “confirming” in Q 5:48 as a seal of approval on the Bible as it existed in the seventh century. The verse itself gives the Qur’an a second role that this reading leaves out.

وَأَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ بِالْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَمُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ

“We have sent down to you the Book in truth, confirming the scripture that came before it and as a guardian over it.”

Q 5:48 · al-Mā’idah

Muṣaddiq: confirming

Muṣaddiq comes from the root ṣ-d-q, truthfulness. To confirm something is to attest that it is true. The phrase li-mā bayna yadayhi, literally “what is between its hands,” is an Arabic idiom for what came before. The Qur’an attests to the truth of earlier revelation: that God spoke to Moses and Jesus, and that what He said was true.

Attesting that a revelation was true is different from attesting that every copy in circulation transmits it without change. The verse makes the first claim. The dilemma needs the second.

Muhaymin: guardian over it

The same sentence calls the Qur’an muhaymin ʿalayhi, “a guardian over it.” Al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn ʿAbbās glossing the word as al-amīn, the trustee, and as shāhid, the witness.1 A trustee or witness stands over a record and decides what in it is authentic. That role only makes sense if parts of the record need judging.

Read together, the two words describe one relationship: the Qur’an confirms what remains of earlier revelation and corrects what has been lost or changed.

The strongest objection: “confirming what is with you”

Elsewhere the Qur’an says it confirms mā maʿakum, “what is with you” Q 2:41, addressing the Children of Israel. Advocates of the dilemma argue that this points to the scripture physically in their hands.

The surrounding verses answer this. The same passage tells them not to “mix truth with falsehood or conceal the truth while you know it” Q 2:42. What the Qur’an confirms is the truth they still have, including what it says about a coming prophet Q 7:157, while it charges them in the next breath with mixing it with falsehood. That is the muhaymin relationship again.

Why this matters for the dilemma

The dilemma offers two options: the Qur’an confirms the Bible wholesale, or rejects it wholesale. Q 5:48 states a third, in one sentence. That is why this verse is the centre of the full response.
