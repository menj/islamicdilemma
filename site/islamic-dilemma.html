Understanding the Islamic Dilemma

The argument as its advocates present it: its premises, the verses it cites, and the choice it asks Muslims to make.

A reply is only worth reading if it answers the argument its opponents actually make. This page states that argument as fairly as we can, before the Muslim response examines it.

The argument in brief

The Islamic Dilemma is used in Christian apologetics to argue that the Qur’an contradicts itself about earlier scripture. Its advocates put it roughly like this:

  1. The Qur’an affirms that the Torah and the Gospel are revealed by God.
  2. The Torah and Gospel it affirms are the Bible held by Jews and Christians in the seventh century.
  3. The Qur’an commands Christians to judge by that scripture, which presupposes it was intact.
  4. If that scripture had been corrupted, God would have failed to preserve His word, which the Qur’an says cannot happen.
  5. Either way, preserved or corrupted, the Qur’an is wrong, so Islam is false.

The verses its advocates cite

Four passages carry most of the weight. We quote them in full because the response depends on reading them closely.

وَلْيَحْكُمْ أَهْلُ الْإِنجِيلِ بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ

“Let the people of the Gospel judge by what God has revealed therein.”

Q 5:47

Read as a command to judge by the Gospel Christians held at the time.

قُلْ يَا أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ لَسْتُمْ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ حَتَّىٰ تُقِيمُوا التَّوْرَاةَ وَالْإِنجِيلَ وَمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُم مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ

“Say: O People of the Book, you have nothing to stand on until you uphold the Torah, the Gospel and what has been sent down to you from your Lord.”

Q 5:68

Read as assuming those books were available to be upheld.

فَإِن كُنتَ فِي شَكٍّ مِّمَّا أَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ فَاسْأَلِ الَّذِينَ يَقْرَءُونَ الْكِتَابَ مِن قَبْلِكَ

“If you are in doubt about what We have sent down to you, ask those who read the scripture before you.”

Q 10:94

Read as sending the Prophet to the scripture of his day as a reliable witness.

لَّا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ

“None can change His words.”

Q 6:115

Read as ruling out any corruption of earlier revelation.

The choice it presents

The argument asks Muslims to pick one side. If they say the Bible is preserved, it contradicts the Qur’an on the crucifixion and the divinity of Jesus. If they say it is corrupted, the Qur’an was wrong to commend it. Either answer, it claims, ends in the same place.

Why the premises matter

A dilemma is valid only if its premises are true and its two options are the only ones available. The response tests both: whether the Qur’an’s “Gospel” is the New Testament, whether 5:47 certifies a text, whether 6:115 speaks about manuscripts, and whether a third position exists that the argument leaves out.
