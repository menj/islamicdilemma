!lead One of the argument's pages gathers every verse in the Qur'an that names the Gospel, the Injil, and makes a simple claim: every reference to the Gospel affirms it, so the Qur'an cannot have regarded it as corrupt. The list is a useful one, and we use it here in the same order. Read verse by verse, it shows something the argument does not mention.

Every one of these verses speaks of the Gospel as God gave it to Jesus. Not one describes four books, apostles or letters. And two of them say things about the Gospel that the argument's own position cannot accept.

## How to read the list {#how}

For each verse we ask three questions. What does it say the Gospel is?

Does it say anything about the state of the Gospel in the Prophet's time? And does it fit the claim that the Qur'an's Gospel is the New Testament? The verses are quoted from the argument's own list, which covers twelve references in nine surahs.

## Q 3:3: revealed, as the Qur'an is revealed {#3-3}

::ayah Q 3:3 | نَزَّلَ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ بِٱلْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًۭا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ وَأَنزَلَ ٱلتَّوْرَىٰةَ وَٱلْإِنجِيلَ | He has sent down to you the Book in truth, confirming what came before it, and He sent down the Torah and the Gospel.

The Gospel is something God "sent down", the same verb used of the Qur'an and the Torah. It is a revelation from God, in the same category as the other two.

The next verse calls the Torah and the Gospel "guidance for people", then names the Qur'an as *al-furqān*, the criterion that distinguishes truth from falsehood {Q 3:4}. The passage affirms the Gospel as revealed, and places the Qur'an as the criterion. It says nothing about four books.

## Q 3:48: taught to Jesus {#3-48}

::ayah Q 3:48 | وَيُعَلِّمُهُ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَٱلتَّوْرَىٰةَ وَٱلْإِنجِيلَ | And He will teach him the Book, wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel.

This is part of the angels' announcement to Mary about the son she will bear. The Gospel is something God will *teach Jesus*.

It is knowledge given to him, and no one wrote it about him after his ministry. This is one of the clearest verses against identifying the Injil with the four Gospels, which were written by others about Jesus decades after the events they describe.

## Q 3:65: revealed after Abraham {#3-65}

> "People of the Book, why do you argue about Abraham, when the Torah and the Gospel were not sent down until after him? Will you not reason?" -- Q 3:65

The verse rebukes Jews and Christians who each claimed Abraham as one of their own. It points out that the books defining their communities came after him, so he could not have followed either.

The Gospel is again "sent down". The verse criticises a claim made by the People of the Book. It endorses nothing they said about their scriptures.

## Q 5:46: given to Jesus, guidance and light {#5-46}

::ayah Q 5:46 | وَءَاتَيْنَـٰهُ ٱلْإِنجِيلَ فِيهِ هُدًۭى وَنُورٌۭ وَمُصَدِّقًۭا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ ٱلتَّوْرَىٰةِ | And We gave him the Gospel, in which is guidance and light, confirming what came before it of the Torah.

"We gave him the Gospel." The verse describes the Gospel at the moment God gave it to Jesus: full of guidance and light, confirming the Torah.

Muslims affirm this exactly as stated. It describes the revelation as given, and says nothing of later copies. It also sits in the passage that begins by condemning those who "distort words from their places" {Q 5:41}, which the argument does not quote.

## Q 5:47, 5:66 and 5:68: judge by it, uphold it {#5-47}

These three verses are the argument's strongest, and we treat them at length in [Judge by it, uphold it](/judge-by-it-uphold-it/). In brief: 5:47 speaks of "what God revealed therein", and one canonical reading makes it a statement of the purpose for which the Gospel was given to Jesus. 5:66 and 5:68 call the People of the Book to uphold the Torah, the Gospel and "what has been sent down to you from your Lord", which includes the Qur'an, and 5:68 predicts that many of them will reject it. None of the three describes the New Testament, and all three sit in a passage that accuses some of the People of the Book of distortion.

## Q 5:110: taught to Jesus, again {#5-110}

> "When God says: Jesus son of Mary, remember My favour to you and to your mother, when I supported you with the Holy Spirit, so that you spoke to people in the cradle and in maturity, and when I taught you the Book, wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel." -- Q 5:110

This verse, addressed to Jesus on the Day of Judgment, repeats that God *taught* him the Gospel. It also lists miracles the Qur'an attributes to Jesus, by God's permission: speaking in the cradle, making a bird of clay, healing the blind and the leper, raising the dead.

Several of these are found in the New Testament; speaking in the cradle and the clay bird are not, though versions of the clay bird appear in early Christian writings outside the New Testament. Whatever one makes of that, the verse again presents the Gospel as something given to Jesus.

## Q 7:157: the Prophet written in it {#7-157}

::ayah Q 7:157 | ٱلَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ ٱلرَّسُولَ ٱلنَّبِىَّ ٱلْأُمِّىَّ ٱلَّذِى يَجِدُونَهُۥ مَكْتُوبًا عِندَهُمْ فِى ٱلتَّوْرَىٰةِ وَٱلْإِنجِيلِ | Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel.

This is the verse the argument's list includes but does not discuss. It says the People of the Book find the Prophet Muhammad "written with them", *ʿindahum*, in the Torah and the Gospel.

If the argument is right that the Qur'an's "Gospel" is simply the New Testament as Christians held it, then this verse says the New Testament describes Muhammad. The argument's advocates do not accept that, and they cannot accept it without accepting Islam.

So the argument faces its own dilemma here. Either the Qur'an's Gospel is identical with the New Testament, in which case the argument must accept that the Qur'an claims the New Testament foretells Muhammad, and must engage with the passages Muslims point to, such as the promise of "another Helper" {John 14:16}.

Or the Qur'an's Gospel differs from the New Testament, in which case the argument's second step collapses. It cannot use the identification to trap Muslims and drop it when 7:157 makes it costly.

## Q 9:111: a promise in all three books {#9-111}

> "God has purchased from the believers their lives and their wealth in exchange for Paradise. ... It is a true promise binding on Him in the Torah, the Gospel and the Qur'an." -- Q 9:111

The verse says that God's promise of Paradise to those who give their lives in His cause is found in all three revelations. The New Testament contains sayings of this kind: "whoever loses his life for my sake will find it" (Matthew 10:39).

The verse describes a teaching common to the three books. It affirms the Gospel's message on that point; it does not certify every page of a later collection.

## Q 48:29: the seed and its shoot {#48-29}

::ayah Q 48:29 | وَمَثَلُهُمْ فِى ٱلْإِنجِيلِ كَزَرْعٍ أَخْرَجَ شَطْـَٔهُۥ فَـَٔازَرَهُۥ فَٱسْتَغْلَظَ فَٱسْتَوَىٰ عَلَىٰ سُوقِهِۦ يُعْجِبُ ٱلزُّرَّاعَ | And their likeness in the Gospel is that of a sown seed that puts out its shoot, strengthens it, grows thick and stands on its stem, delighting the sowers.

This verse describes the believers' likeness in the Gospel as a seed that sprouts and grows strong. The Gospels contain parables of exactly this shape.

Jesus describes the kingdom of God as seed that sprouts and grows, "first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain in the ear" {Mark 4:26–29}, and as a mustard seed that grows larger than all the garden plants {Mark 4:30–32}. The Qur'an here refers to the content of the Gospel, the teaching of Jesus, and finds it consistent with its own description of the believers. That is the Qur'an recognising the Injil within what Christians held, which is exactly the Muslim position.

## Q 57:27: what God gave, and what they added {#57-27}

::ayah Q 57:27 | وَقَفَّيْنَا بِعِيسَى ٱبْنِ مَرْيَمَ وَءَاتَيْنَـٰهُ ٱلْإِنجِيلَ وَجَعَلْنَا فِى قُلُوبِ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوهُ رَأْفَةًۭ وَرَحْمَةًۭ وَرَهْبَانِيَّةً ٱبْتَدَعُوهَا مَا كَتَبْنَـٰهَا عَلَيْهِمْ | We sent Jesus son of Mary after them and gave him the Gospel, and placed in the hearts of those who followed him compassion and mercy. But monasticism they invented; We did not prescribe it for them.

This is the last verse in the argument's list, and it is the most revealing. It begins by affirming the Gospel God gave Jesus and the compassion and mercy God placed in his followers' hearts.

Then it distinguishes: "monasticism they invented; We did not prescribe it for them." It goes on: "they did not observe it as it should be observed ... and many of them are defiantly disobedient."

The verse draws precisely the line the dilemma says the Qur'an never draws. It separates what God gave through Jesus from what his followers added on their own initiative.

The argument quotes this verse as an affirmation of the Gospel. It is an affirmation of the Gospel as given, together with a criticism of what was added afterwards.

## What the whole list shows {#shows}

Taken together, the twelve references form a consistent picture.

- Every one that describes the Gospel's origin says God sent it down, gave it to Jesus, or taught it to him. None says it was written by Jesus' followers.
- None mentions four Gospels, Matthew, Mark, Luke or John, or any letter or apostle.
- Several are set inside passages that accuse some of the People of the Book of distortion, concealment or forgetting {Q 5:13–15, 41}.
- Two describe the Gospel's content: one in terms that match the parables of Jesus {Q 48:29}, one in terms that Christians reject {Q 7:157}.
- One explicitly separates what God gave from what Jesus' followers invented {Q 57:27}.

The argument is right that the Qur'an never speaks ill of the Gospel. It never speaks ill of the Torah either.

Muslims do not speak ill of them. What the Qur'an criticises, consistently, is what some people did with what they were given. A list that collects the praise and leaves out the criticism is not a neutral survey.

## Conclusion {#conclusion}

"Every reference to the Gospel affirms it" is true, and it is no problem for Muslims, because every one of those references affirms the Gospel as God gave it to Jesus. None identifies it with the New Testament. One claims the Gospel foretells Muhammad, which the argument cannot accept, and another separates God's gift from human additions, which is the Muslim position stated in a single verse.
