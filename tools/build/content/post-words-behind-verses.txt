!lead Arguments about the Islamic Dilemma are usually argued from English translations. This article goes to the Arabic, using the word-by-word grammar of the Quranic Arabic Corpus, and asks of each verse in question what the grammar settles and what it leaves open.

The [Quranic Arabic Corpus](https://corpus.quran.com/) is an open linguistic project of the University of Leeds, begun by Kais Dukes, that tags every word of the Qur'an for root, form and grammatical function. The notes below paraphrase its analysis and link to its pages; they do not reproduce its data. Each verse is limited to the words that bear on the polemic.

## What the grammar of Q 5:48 says {#muhaymin}

::ayah Q 5:48 | وَأَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ بِٱلْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًۭا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ وَمُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ ۖ | To thee We sent the Scripture in truth, confirming the scripture that came before it, and guarding it in safety…

The advocates of the dilemma read this verse as an endorsement of the Gospel in the hands of Christians. The grammar gives three points against that reading.

First, *muṣaddiqan* ("confirming") and *muhayminan* ("guarding") are both form II active participles. They stand side by side in the accusative, so both describe the same thing: the Book sent down to the Prophet. The Qur'an is at once the confirmer of what came before and its guardian.

Second, *muhayminan* comes from the root *h-m-n*, which occurs twice in the Qur'an, both times as this participle: here, and in Q 59:23, where *al-muhaymin* is one of the names of God. The word implies a role: something to guard against change, and something that can tell the genuine text from what was added.

Third, the next clause is an imperative, *fa-uḥkum* ("so judge"), followed by a prohibition, *wa-lā tattabiʿ* ("and do not follow"). The Book is the standard by which the Prophet judges, as [what Q 5:48 confirms](/what-does-quran-5-48-confirm/) sets out at length.

## The verb behind taḥrīf {#harrafa}

The root *ḥ-r-f* occurs six times in the Qur'an. Four are the same verb, a form II imperfect, *yuḥarrifūna*, and the Corpus glosses it as "to distort."

::ayah Q 2:75 | وَقَدْ كَانَ فَرِيقٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَـٰمَ ٱللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُۥ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ | Seeing that a party of them heard the Word of Allah, and perverted it knowingly after they understood it.

Three observations follow from the data.

The verb is always imperfect, a form that often describes an action as ongoing or repeated. Arabic also uses it for narrative present, so the form alone does not prove a habit. The four verses are Q 2:75, 4:46, 5:13 and 5:41.

The grammatical subject is always a group among the Children of Israel. In Q 4:46 and 5:41 the Qur'an names them with the verb *hādū* ("became Jews"). In Q 2:75 it is "a party of them," and in Q 5:13 it is those who broke the covenant.

Three of the four verses add a phrase about place, *ʿan mawāḍiʿihi* or *min baʿdi mawāḍiʿihi* ("from its places"), and Q 2:75 adds *min baʿdi mā ʿaqalūhu* ("after they understood it"). The object in all four is *al-kalima* or the pronoun that stands for it: "the word." The grammar does not say how the word was moved. Q 2:75 adds that it was done knowingly, since the verse ends *wahum yaʿlamūna* ("while they know").

The data also has a limit. None of the four verbs has the Christians as its subject. The Qur'an describes Christians with a different verb, taken up next.

## Forgetting, and who does it {#nasu}

Q 5:13 says of the Children of Israel *wa-nasū ḥaẓẓan* ("and they forgot a portion"). Q 5:14 says of "those who say we are Christians" *fa-nasū ḥaẓẓan* with the same phrase, using the perfect form of *n-s-y*. The Corpus tags both as third person masculine plural perfect verbs.

So the Qur'an charges Christians with forgetting a portion of what they were reminded of, in words that match its charge against the Children of Israel.

It does not use the verb of *taḥrīf* of them in these verses. [How the Qur'an describes alteration](/how-quran-describes-alteration/) gathers the other descriptions.

## What Q 5:68 asks {#iqama}

::ayah Q 5:68 | قُلْ يَـٰٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ لَسْتُمْ عَلَىٰ شَىْءٍ حَتَّىٰ تُقِيمُوا۟ ٱلتَّوْرَىٰةَ وَٱلْإِنجِيلَ وَمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُم مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ ۗ | Say: "O People of the Book! ye have no ground to stand upon unless ye stand fast by the Law, the Gospel, and all the revelation that has come to you from your Lord."

The verb *tuqīmū* ("you establish") is a form IV imperfect in the subjunctive, governed by *ḥattā* ("until"). Its objects are three, joined by *wa*: *al-tawrāta*, *al-injīla* and *mā unzila ilaykum min rabbikum* ("what was sent down to you from your Lord"). The same verb takes all three.

Two points follow.

The command is addressed to the People of the Book of the Prophet's day, and the condition is concrete: they stand on nothing until they establish.

The third object, "what was sent down to you" (*ilaykum*, plural), uses the same verb *unzila* as the next clause, "sent down to you" (*ilayka*, singular), which is the Qur'an sent to the Prophet.

Many commentators read the plural as the Qur'an too, which would place establishing the Torah and the Gospel in one act with accepting it. The grammar allows that reading without forcing it. [Judge by it, uphold it](/judge-by-it-uphold-it/) reads the passage in full.

## Preservation: Q 15:9 and the pronoun {#hafiz}

In Q 15:9, *nazzalnā* ("We have sent down") is a form II perfect, and the object is *al-dhikr*, "the Reminder." The guardians are *ḥāfiẓūna*, and the pronoun *lahu* ("of it") points back to *al-dhikr*. The verse names no earlier book as the object of the guarding. [The preservation article](/does-quran-15-9-depend-on-6-115/) asks what follows from that.

## What the data settles {#settles}

The grammar settles less than either side hopes, and more than silence would suggest.

It settles that the Qur'an presents itself as both confirmer and guardian of earlier scripture, that it uses a verb of distortion four times of a group among the Children of Israel, and that it charges Christians with forgetting a portion. It also settles that one verb in Q 5:68 governs the Torah, the Gospel and what was sent down to them, and that the command is conditional.

It does not settle which manuscript of which Gospel a Christian holds today. That is a question for the manuscripts, and the Qur'an gives the standard (a guardian, a criterion) by which to ask it.
