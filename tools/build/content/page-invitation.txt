!lead This page is addressed to Christians, as the Qur'an addresses them: as People of the Book, who already know Abraham, Moses and Jesus, and who already believe in the God of Abraham. It is an invitation, and it is meant seriously. We do not doubt your sincerity.

We are asking you to consider whether the faith you were taught is the faith Jesus taught.

## A common word {#common-word}

::ayah Q 3:64 | قُلْ يَـٰٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ تَعَالَوْا۟ إِلَىٰ كَلِمَةٍۢ سَوَآءٍۭ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمْ أَلَّا نَعْبُدَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ وَلَا نُشْرِكَ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا | Say: "O People of the Book! come to common terms as between us and you: That we worship none but Allah; that we associate no partners with him…

This verse is the Qur'an's invitation to Christians, and it begins with what we share. Christians and Muslims agree that there is one God, the Creator of all things, who spoke to Abraham and Moses, sent the prophets, and will judge the world.

We agree that Jesus was born of the virgin Mary, that he was the Messiah, that he worked miracles, and that he will return. That is a great deal of common ground. The disagreement lies in one place: whether anything other than God shares in His divinity.

## What shirk is {#shirk}

The Qur'an's word for associating anything with God in His divinity is *shirk*. It means giving to something created what belongs to God alone: worship, prayer addressed to it as divine, belief that it shares God's nature or power. The Qur'an calls it "a tremendous wrong" {Q 31:13}, and it is the one sin God says He will not forgive if a person dies upon it, while He forgives anything less than it for whom He wills:

::ayah Q 4:48 | إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَن يُشْرَكَ بِهِۦ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ ۚ | Allah forgiveth not that partners should be set up with Him; but He forgiveth anything else, to whom He pleaseth…

We say this plainly because it is the most important thing a Muslim can tell a Christian. It is not an insult. It is a warning, given out of concern, about the one thing the Qur'an says matters most.

## Why the Trinity is shirk {#trinity}

From the Qur'an's point of view, worshipping Jesus as God, or the Holy Spirit as God, gives created beings what belongs to God alone. Christians understand the Trinity as one God in three persons, and they do not see it as worshipping three gods.

We do not misrepresent that belief. But the Qur'an addresses the doctrine directly, and it does not accept it:

::ayah Q 5:72 | لَقَدْ كَفَرَ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلْمَسِيحُ ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ ۖ وَقَالَ ٱلْمَسِيحُ يَـٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ رَبِّى وَرَبَّكُمْ ۖ | They do blaspheme who say: "Allah is Christ the son of Mary." But said Christ: "O Children of Israel! worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord."

It tells those who say God is "the third of three" to stop {Q 5:73}. It tells the People of the Book, "do not say 'three'; desist, it is better for you; God is only one God" {Q 4:171}. And it says that the Messiah "would never disdain to be a servant of God" {Q 4:172}.

The Qur'an's Holy Spirit is not a person of God. It is the angel Gabriel, who brought revelation: "Say: the Holy Spirit brought it down from your Lord in truth" {Q 16:102}. God is one, without partners, without a son, without a second or third person sharing His nature.

## What Jesus said about God {#jesus-on-god}

You do not have to take the Qur'an's word alone for this. The Gospels preserve sayings in which Jesus speaks as a servant of the One God, and they are worth reading slowly.

> "The most important is, 'Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one.'" -- Mark 12:29 · ESV

> "Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone." -- Mark 10:18 · ESV

> "And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." -- John 17:3 · ESV

> "I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God." -- John 20:17 · ESV

"The Lord is one." "No one is good except God alone."

"You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." "My God and your God."

In his own recorded words, Jesus distinguishes himself from God, calls the Father "the only true God", calls God "my God", and describes himself as the one sent. That is the Qur'an's account of Jesus.

The doctrine of the Trinity was given its classic form by church councils in the fourth century, at Nicaea in 325 and Constantinople in 381, three hundred years after him. Christians point to Jesus' command to baptise "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19).

But naming three together is not the same as saying the three are one God, and nowhere in the Gospels does Jesus say that. The words he does say, quoted above, point the other way.

## The message of Jesus, and the theology built around it {#paul}

Much of what Christians believe about salvation, that Jesus' death pays for the sins of the world and that faith in it alone saves, comes from the letters of Paul. Paul never followed Jesus during his ministry.

He says his gospel came to him by private revelation, with no word from Jesus' disciples (Galatians 1:11–12), and he records that he opposed Peter to his face (Galatians 2:11). When Jesus was asked how to enter eternal life, he said: "If you would enter life, keep the commandments" (Matthew 19:17).

We invite you to follow the message of Jesus rather than the theology later built around him. [Paul and the message of Jesus](/paul-and-message-of-jesus/) sets out the evidence from the New Testament itself.

## Repent, and come home {#repent}

After warning against calling God "the third of three", the Qur'an asks a question that is also an invitation:

::ayah Q 5:74 | أَفَلَا يَتُوبُونَ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَيَسْتَغْفِرُونَهُۥ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ | Why turn they not to Allah, and seek His forgiveness? For Allah is Oft-forgiving, Most Merciful.

Repentance in Islam is turning back to God directly. It needs no intermediary and no sacrifice.

Whoever turns from *shirk* to *tawḥīd* is forgiven what came before: "tell those who disbelieve that if they stop, what is past will be forgiven them" {Q 8:38}. Whatever you have believed until now, the door is open.

We invite you, sincerely, to repent of associating Jesus or the Spirit with God, to worship God alone as Jesus did, and to come to Islam.

## What you keep, and what changes {#keep}

Becoming Muslim does not mean leaving Jesus behind. Muslims love Jesus, honour him as the Messiah and one of the greatest prophets, and await his return.

They honour his mother Mary, whom the Qur'an says God "chose and purified, and chose above the women of the worlds" {Q 3:42}, and after whom a whole surah is named. You would keep your belief in the God of Abraham, in the prophets, in the Torah and the Gospel as God revealed them, in angels, in judgment and in eternal life.

What changes is the object of worship. You would pray to God alone, with no one between you and Him.

The last prophet, Muhammad, confirmed Jesus' message and completed it, and God promised to guard the Qur'an he received. With them you would pray, fast, give charity and live by God's guidance, as Jesus himself did.

## Questions Christians often ask {#questions}

Christians considering Islam often ask the same questions, and they deserve direct answers.

**Would I have to reject Jesus?** No. A Muslim who rejected Jesus would cease to be a Muslim.

You would believe in Jesus as the Qur'an presents him: the Messiah, born of a virgin, a word from God, a prophet who worked miracles and will return. What you would stop doing is worshipping him.

**What about the cross?** The Qur'an says that God raised Jesus to Himself, and that those who sought his death saw only an appearance of it {Q 4:157–158}. Muslims believe Jesus was saved from his enemies by God.

This is a real difference from Christian belief, and we do not hide it. But it is worth noticing that the Gospels themselves record Jesus praying that the cup might pass from him (Matthew 26:39), and that God answers the prayers of the righteous.

**What would happen to my sins?** They would be forgiven. The Qur'an promises that those who turn from *shirk* to the worship of God alone are forgiven what came before {Q 8:38}, and the Prophet taught that entering Islam wipes away the past. From then on, like every Muslim, you would turn to God directly for forgiveness whenever you fell short.

**What about my family?** Islam commands kindness to parents and family, including those of other faiths. The Qur'an tells believers to keep company with their parents "in this world with kindness" even when they disagree about God {Q 31:15}. Becoming Muslim should make you a better son, daughter, husband, wife or parent, not a worse one.

## How to take the first step {#first-step}

If you are ready, becoming Muslim is simple. You bear witness, sincerely, that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His messenger. You may add, if you wish, words that state what you are turning to:

::shahada turn

!callout "I turn back to God alone. I believe that Jesus, son of Mary, is His servant and messenger, and that Muhammad is His servant and final messenger. I bear witness that there is no god but God, and that Muhammad is the messenger of God."

[Why Islam?](/why-islam/#becoming) explains what the declaration means and what comes after it.

If you are not ready, keep reading. There is "no compulsion in religion" {Q 2:256}.

Read the Gospels and the Qur'an side by side. Ask God, in your own words, to show you the truth.

Speak to Muslims you trust. And take your time: God is patient, and He answers those who call on Him.
