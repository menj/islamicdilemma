!lead A reply is only complete when it meets the reply to it. The Muslim answer to the Islamic Dilemma has been put to Christian apologists and scholars, and they have responded. This page sets out the seven answers we hear most often, in the strongest form each can take, says what is right in each, and says what the evidence adds.

If we have misstated one, tell us through the [contact form](/contact/) and we will correct it.

## 1. "The Qur'an confirmed the Bible of the seventh century; any corruption came later" {#later}

**The reply.** Whatever the Qur'an says about corruption, it affirmed the scriptures that Jews and Christians held in the Prophet's time. The Bible we have now descends from those, and the manuscripts show it has not changed since. So the Qur'an's confirmation applies to our Bible.

**What is right.** The Qur'an does refer to books its hearers held, as the phrase *ʿindahum*, "in their possession", shows {Q 7:157}. And the New Testament manuscripts do show a stable text from the fourth century.

**What the evidence adds.** The manuscripts are silent on the three centuries before the fourth, and there are almost no papyri to compare. They also show later additions: the longer ending of Mark, the woman taken in adultery, and the "three witnesses" of 1 John 5:7.

And "in their possession" certifies where a book was. It says nothing about what the book contained.

The Qur'an's own word for what the Christians did with the Gospel is that they *forgot* part of it {Q 5:14}, and that loss is not something later manuscripts could show. See [What the New Testament manuscripts can and cannot show](/what-new-testament-manuscripts-can-and-cannot-show/) and [The Qur'an argues with its hearers](/quran-argues-with-its-hearers/).

## 2. "Muslims claim corruption but cannot show where" {#where}

**The reply.** If the text was corrupted, point to the verse, the manuscript and the date. Muslims name no variant, no reading, and no uncorrupted Gospel to set against the one we have.

**What is right.** Vague claims of corruption are not evidence, and some Muslims make them.

**What the evidence adds.** The Muslim scholars of the first centuries did name places, and modern textual criticism names many more: the endings of Mark, John 7:53–8:11, 1 John 5:7–8, 1 Timothy 3:16, Hebrews 2:9. In the Hebrew Bible, the Dead Sea Scrolls show Isaiah, Samuel and Jeremiah in forms that differ from the Masoretic text.

And the Qur'an's claim is not that a different complete Gospel exists in a library. It is that the Injīl was the revelation given to Jesus, which the canonical Gospels preserve in part.

The request for "the uncorrupted version" presupposes a position Muslims do not hold. See [Where is the Injil?](/injil-where-is-it/) and [The Hebrew Bible before the Masoretic text](/hebrew-bible-before-masoretic-text/).

## 3. "The Qur'an tells Christians to judge by the Gospel they have" {#judge}

**The reply.** Q 5:47 commands the people of the Gospel to judge by it, and Q 5:68 says they have "no ground" unless they uphold it. The Qur'an would not command what was impossible. So the Gospel they had was reliable.

**What is right.** A command to follow a scripture implies that it can be followed.

**What the evidence adds.** Both verses speak of "what God has revealed therein", and "what has been revealed to you from your Lord", which is more than a book list. Both belong to a passage on legal judgment, in which the Qur'an is immediately named *muhaymin* over the earlier books {Q 5:48}.

Verse 68 ends by saying what was sent to Muhammad will only increase many of them in rebellion, which is not a certificate for their text. The same structure appears in Matthew 23:3, which no one reads as endorsing every teaching of the Pharisees. See [Judge by it, uphold it](/judge-by-it-uphold-it/).

## 4. "If the text could be corrupted, God did not keep His word" {#kept}

**The reply.** Q 6:115 and 18:27 say none can change God's words. If the Bible was corrupted, the Qur'an is wrong, or God failed.

**What is right.** If those verses did promise the preservation of every earlier book, the objection would stand.

**What the evidence adds.** The commentators, from al-Ṭabarī to al-Rāzī, read "His words" as God's decrees and promises, and never as the wording of scrolls. The Qur'an itself describes people changing what God said, in the same breath, and says the Torah was *entrusted* to custodians while the Qur'an is guarded by God {Q 5:44 · 15:9}.

A promise to guard one revelation does not promise to guard all. See ["None can change His words"](/none-can-change-his-words/) and [Does the Qur'an's preservation depend on "none can change His words"?](/does-quran-15-9-depend-on-6-115/)

## 5. "If the corruption was only in meaning, the text is still the Bible's" {#meaning}

**The reply.** Many classical Muslims said the text was intact and only misread. Then the Bible's text is the true Gospel, and the Qur'an confirms it.

**What is right.** Many classical scholars did say that, and it is a real Muslim position.

**What the evidence adds.** The classical position was not that the text was intact and the doctrines were wrong by misreading only. It was that the Qur'an is the criterion, accepting what agrees with it and rejecting what does not, as Ryan Schaffner's study shows.

In that method the Qur'an judges a doctrine such as the crucifixion or divine sonship, whatever the state of the text. The Qur'an also has a lot to say about what Christians *did* with their scripture, and it calls itself the arbiter over what survives. See [What classical Muslim scholars actually said about taḥrīf](/what-classical-muslim-scholars-said-about-tahrif/).

## 6. "Your method is circular: the Qur'an judges, so the Bible counts for nothing" {#circular}

**The reply.** Muslims accept what agrees with the Qur'an and reject the rest. That makes the Qur'an the only authority and the Bible mere ornament.

**What is right.** The Qur'an does claim the last word. That is what *muhaymin* means.

**What the evidence adds.** Every later revelation judges the earlier one. Christians read the Hebrew Bible through Christ, setting aside the food laws, the priesthood and the temple sacrifices.

If that is legitimate, so is the Muslim reading. The objection, put evenly, is an objection to the idea of a later revelation at all, and Christianity is that idea.

Celsus and Julian made the same objection against the Christians, and the Christian answers to them are the Muslim answers now. See [Two standards](/two-standards-method-behind-dilemma/).

## 7. "Paul's letters are scripture, so the Bible's testimony stands" {#paul}

**The reply.** Even if the Gospels are set aside, Paul preached Christ crucified and risen, and his letters are the earliest Christian writings. Muslims who reject them reject apostolic testimony.

**What is right.** Paul's letters are the earliest Christian texts we have, and they show that belief in Christ's death and resurrection is very early.

**What the evidence adds.** Islam objects to Paul's standing. The Prophet taught that there was no prophet between Jesus and Muhammad, and Paul claimed revelation, which is a prophet's office.

The Pastoral Letters are doubted by most critical scholars. And to prove Paul's authority from Paul's own claim, from Acts, and from 2 Peter is circular. See [Paul and the message of Jesus](/paul-and-message-of-jesus/) and [The proof-texts, sorted](/proof-texts-sorted/).

## What a fair exchange would look like {#fair}

None of these seven replies is foolish, and several contain a real point. A fair exchange would take them one at a time, concede what is right in each, and look at the evidence for the rest.

It would apply to both books the same standards of text, authority and reading. And it would let each side say what would change its mind.

If a Christian reader can do that, the conversation is worth having. The [ten honest questions](/honest-questions-for-christians/) are put in that spirit.
