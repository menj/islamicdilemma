!lead Bassam Zawadi's article "Evidence That Islam Teaches That There Was Textual Corruption of the Christian and Jewish Scriptures" gathers verses, hadith, reports from the Companions and the words of later scholars. This page lists every one of them. Each entry gives the reference, what the source says and whether I could find the wording in the cited work.

## How to read the list {#how}

Every entry ends with one of three statuses.

- Checked: I found the Arabic text, or the passage it translates, in the cited work on Shamela.
- Partly checked: I found the work, or the same passage in a related work, but not the exact wording or page cited.
- Not checked: I could not reach the source, and the reference is as Zawadi gives it.

Gradings of hadith (sound, good, weak) are Zawadi's report of other scholars' verdicts. I have not re-graded anything. Page numbers follow the editions on Shamela, so they can differ from the editions Zawadi used.

The list includes sources that favour the view that the change was in meaning as well as those that speak of changed wording. The articles on [how the Qur'an describes alteration](/how-quran-describes-alteration/) and [what classical scholars said about taḥrīf](/what-classical-muslim-scholars-said-about-tahrif/) set out the site's own reading.

## The words Torah, Gospel and taḥrīf {#words}

Zawadi starts by fixing how the Qur'an, the hadith and the scholars use these words.

- Ibn Kathīr, on Q 5:110, says that "the Torah" there is what was revealed to Mūsā ibn ʿImrān, and that in a hadith the word can carry a wider sense. *Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr*, vol. 3, p. 223 (al-Salāma ed.). Checked.
- A passage says that "Torah", "Gospel", "Psalms" and "Qur'an" name a particular book at times and the whole class of books at others. Zawadi gives it to Ibn Taymiyyah. I found it in Ibn al-Qayyim, *Hidāyat al-ḥayārā*, p. 183, where it comments on the words "this is in the Torah". Partly checked.
- Camilla Adang, "Torah", *Encyclopaedia of the Qurʾān*, vol. 5, p. 300: after the Qur'an the word often names the whole Hebrew Bible and Jewish writings outside it. Not checked.
- al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī, *al-Mufradāt fī gharīb al-Qurʾān*, ed. M. Aḥmad Khalaf Allāh (Cairo, 1965), vol. 1, pp. 122 f.: Zawadi gives the root sense of *taḥrīf* as leaning from the pen in one direction, or twisting words to suit one's desire. Not checked. Lane's *Lexicon* gives the same root sense of turning a thing from its proper way, under *ḥ-r-f* (form 1).
- al-Shahrastānī: *taḥrīf* means changing the written word for another to give it a corrupt meaning. Zawadi's reference reads "op. cit., vol. 3, p. 11" and does not name the work. Not checked.
- Ibn Taymiyyah, in *al-ʿAqīda al-Ḥamawiyya*, divides *taḥrīf* into three kinds: changing a word so that the meaning changes, changing a word with no change of meaning, and changing the meaning of a word. Zawadi concludes that *taḥrīf* can mean changing letters. Not checked.
- Abū l-Walīd al-Bājī, *al-Muntaqā sharḥ al-Muwaṭṭaʾ*, vol. 7, p. 280, on the Jews who greeted the Prophet with «a|السام عليكم» in place of «a|السلام عليكم»: he says they "distort words from their places" as God described. Checked. The example is a spoken greeting, and Zawadi uses it to show that *taḥrīf* can change letters.

## The Qur'an {#quran}

Zawadi builds on two verses and answers one objection.

- Q 2:79: "Then woe to those who write the Book with their own hands, and then say: 'This is from Allah,' to traffic with it for miserable price!" Zawadi reads it as a plain charge that some Jews wrote a book and passed it off as God's. The verse names those who write, and it does not say how much of any book they wrote.
- Q 3:113–114 and Q 3:199: the objection is that the Qur'an also praises some of the People of the Book. Zawadi answers that these were believers in Muhammad, citing Ibn Kathīr and the *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn*, and that being praised does not mean they held a complete text. Not checked.
- Q 4:157: Zawadi argues that the verse denies the killing of the Messiah while the Gospels teach the crucifixion, so the Qur'an indirectly says the Gospels are corrupted. He adds that no one in the Prophet's time, whether Christian converts or opponents, is recorded as objecting. The site answers the verse in [“So it was made to appear to them”: Q 4:157 and the history of the crucifixion](/quran-4-157-and-history-of-crucifixion/).
- Q 9:100: Zawadi quotes the verse that promises Paradise to the first Emigrants and Helpers as the ground for giving weight to the Companions' understanding.

## The Prophet's own statements {#prophet}

These are sayings and actions reported of the Prophet, with the sources Zawadi gives for each.

- Muʿādh ibn Jabal saw Christians prostrate to bishops and Jews to rabbis in Syria, and the Prophet said: "They lied about their prophets just as they distorted their Book." Checked: *Musnad Aḥmad*, vol. 32, p. 149 (al-Risāla ed.), where the Prophet's words include «a|كذبوا على أنبيائهم كما حرفوا كتابهم». Zawadi adds al-Ḥākim, *al-Mustadrak*, who calls it sound by the standard of the two Ṣaḥīḥs; al-Ṭabarānī, *al-Muʿjam al-kabīr* 8/31, with a weak narrator in that chain; and al-Haythamī, *Majmaʿ al-zawāʾid* 4/312. Those three are not checked. Zawadi reads the comparison as an invention of false statements.
- "The Children of Israel wrote a book, followed it and left the Torah." Checked: al-Suyūṭī, *al-Jāmiʿ al-ṣaghīr*, no. 3807, from Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī, assigned to al-Ṭabarānī. Zawadi names al-Ṭabarānī's *al-Muʿjam al-awsaṭ* and gives al-Albānī's grading of it as sound, *al-Silsila al-ṣaḥīḥa* no. 2832. The grading is not checked. Zawadi answers the objection that this means the Talmud by saying the Jews still follow the Pentateuch.
- "When time grew long and their hearts hardened, they invented a book of their own, which their hearts and tongues found pleasing." Partly checked. I found these words as a saying of Ibn Masʿūd (*Taṭrīz Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn*, p. 382). I did not find the Prophetic ascription that Zawadi gives, which is al-Bayhaqī, *Shuʿab al-īmān*, with al-Albānī's grading of sound (*Silsila* no. 2694).
- Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh: the Jews brought a man and a woman who had committed adultery, and the Prophet asked the two most learned of them what they found in the Torah. Checked: *Sunan Abī Dāwūd*, vol. 6, p. 502 (al-Arnaʾūṭ ed.); Zawadi cites hadith 3862 in his source and al-Albānī's grading at 4452. Zawadi notes that Leviticus 20:10 has no four-witness rule and infers that this part of the Torah was changed. The report shows what the two scholars said the Torah contained. It does not say the text had been altered.
- al-Fulṭān ibn ʿĀṣim: the Prophet asks a man whether he reads the Torah and the Gospel, and the man says that he finds the Prophet's like and his community, with seventy thousand entering Paradise without reckoning. Checked: *Ṣaḥīḥ Mawārid al-ẓamʾān*, vol. 2, p. 311. Zawadi gives the grading as sound (no. 1765) and infers from the absence of this detail in the Bible now in use that it was removed.
- The Jews will split into 72 sects, and this community into 73, all in the Fire except the one on "what I and my Companions are upon". Zawadi cites al-Tirmidhī (5/62), al-Ḥākim (1/128), al-ʿIrāqī, *Takhrīj al-Iḥyāʾ* 3/199, and al-Albānī, *Silsila* no. 204, as his ground for treating the Companions as an authority. Not checked.

## The Companions {#companions}

Zawadi's reading of each report rests on one step: a detail the Companion reports from the Torah or the Gospel does not appear in the Bible today, so it must have been taken out.

- ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb and a Jew: ʿUmar ruled for the Jew in a dispute, and the Jew said that the Torah says a judge who rules by the truth has an angel on each side. Checked: the report is in Ibn al-Athīr, *Jāmiʿ al-uṣūl*, vol. 10, p. 170. Zawadi gives al-Mundhirī's grading as sound (*al-Targhīb wa-l-tarhīb* 3/188), which is not checked.
- ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd: Sūrat al-Mulk "is in the Torah" and protects from the punishment of the grave. Checked: the wording is in al-Thaʿlabī, *al-Kashf wa-l-bayān*, vol. 27, p. 84. Zawadi gives al-Mundhirī, *al-Targhīb* 2/320, and al-Albānī, *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Targhīb* no. 1475, for the grading, which is not checked.
- Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān said to ʿUthmān: "Save this community before they differ about the Book as the Jews and the Christians did." Zawadi cites *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* from Anas, and reads the remark as showing that Ḥudhayfah thought the earlier scriptures had lost their textual integrity. He adds the gloss of Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī, *Mirqāt al-mafātīḥ*, that their differences over the Torah and the Gospel went as far as distorting, adding and omitting. Partly checked: I found the same gloss in al-Mubārakfūrī, *Mirʿāt al-mafātīḥ*, vol. 7, p. 326.
- ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām, a learned rabbi before his conversion, asked the Prophet three questions that only a prophet would know. Zawadi cites *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* in two places (Khan numbering, vol. 4, book 55, no. 546, and vol. 6, book 60, no. 7), and infers that the answers must have come from the Torah and are absent from the Bible today. Not checked as to numbering. The report shows that he accepted the Prophet's answers, and it does not say they were in the Torah.
- ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām at Nahāwand: he told a Jewish leader that he knew the leader's book better than he did, and recited the Torah to him for three days. Checked: Ibn Ḥajar, *al-Maṭālib al-ʿāliya*, vol. 15, p. 658; also Ibn Abī Shayba, *al-Muṣannaf*, vol. 6, p. 558. Zawadi cites Ibn Ḥajar's grading of the report as sound and as ending at the Companion (no. 218 in his edition), which is not checked.
- ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, asked by ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār about the Prophet's description in the Torah, quotes "a guardian of the unlettered, you are My servant and My messenger". Checked: *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* (al-Bughā ed.), vol. 2, p. 747. Zawadi (Khan, vol. 3, book 34, no. 335) says that Isaiah 42 lacks several of these traits.

Ibn ʿAbbās is the Companion Zawadi spends most time on, and the entries below follow his order.

- The Prophet's prayers for Ibn ʿAbbās to be given knowledge of the Book and understanding of religion: *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* (Khan, vol. 1, book 3, no. 75, and vol. 1, book 4, no. 145) and *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* (book 31, no. 6055, as Zawadi numbers it). Not checked.
- Ibn ʿAbbās on asking the People of the Book: "How can you ask the People of the Book about their books, when your Book is the newest news from God, and God has told you that they changed what God wrote and altered the Book with their hands?" Checked: *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, hadith 2539 in the al-Bughā edition (vol. 2, p. 953); Zawadi gives Khan vol. 9, book 93, nos. 613 and 614.
- Ibn Ḥazm says this is the soundest chain to Ibn ʿAbbās "which is exactly our view" and that the Companions did not differ on it (*al-Fiṣal*, vol. 2, p. 3). Not checked.
- *Tanwīr al-miqbās min tafsīr Ibn ʿAbbās*, on Q 2:79: those "who write the Scripture with their hands" change the description of Muhammad in the Book. Not checked. The work is a compilation attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās.
- al-Māwardī, *al-Nukat wa-l-ʿuyūn*, on Q 2:79, gives Ibn ʿAbbās's reading of "the unlettered" as people who wrote a book with their own hands and told the ignorant it was from God. Checked: vol. 1, p. 150.
- On Q 2:42, the words «a|وَقَوْل اِبْن عَبَّاس أَصْوَب» ("the truth" is the Torah and "the falsehood" is what they wrote with their own hands). Zawadi gives them to al-Ṭabarī. Checked, with a correction: they are the comment of al-Qurṭubī in his own commentary (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, vol. 1, p. 342), who writes «a|قلت» ("I say") before preferring Ibn ʿAbbās. They are not al-Ṭabarī's.
- Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, on Q 2:75, quotes al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār as saying that Ibn ʿAbbās held that additions and omissions had been made. Not checked.
- Ibn ʿAbbās: "The kings after ʿĪsā ibn Maryam substituted the Torah and the Gospel", and believers among them who read the Torah were threatened with death unless they stopped reading it except for what had been substituted. Checked: *Sunan al-Nasāʾī*, vol. 8, p. 424 (al-Risāla ed.). Zawadi gives al-Albānī's grading as sound, which is not checked, and infers that the substituted copies spread more widely.
- The remark in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* that no one can remove the wording of a book of God but that they distort it by wrong interpretation, set after a saying of Ibn ʿAbbās. Zawadi gives three replies: it has no complete chain, quoting Ibn Ḥajar, *Fatḥ al-Bārī*, that he did not find it joined to Ibn ʿAbbās and that many of "our colleagues" held the books to be altered; al-ʿAynī, *ʿUmdat al-qārī* 1/9, says al-Bukhārī often relates sayings without a chain; and the saying could mean that the text kept in the Preserved Tablet cannot change, which he supports with Ibn Kathīr on Q 85:22. Not checked. The [alteration article](/how-quran-describes-alteration/) sets out the remark and notes that scholars discuss whose words it is.

## The early Muslims {#early}

These reports come from the generations after the Companions, and several are from converts from Judaism and Christianity.

- Kaʿb al-Aḥbār, a rabbi who became a Muslim, quotes lines he says are in the Torah: "Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, My chosen servant, neither harsh nor coarse", and a second line on the community who praise God and watch the sun for prayer. Checked: al-Dārimī, *Musnad*, vol. 1, p. 45 (al-Zahrānī ed.). Zawadi adds al-Ḥākim, *al-Mustadrak* 2/614, Ibn ʿAsākir, and al-Albānī's grading of sound (*Silsila* no. 2458), which are not checked. He says the lines go beyond Isaiah 42.
- Kaʿb, in another report: "I am sending down to you, Muhammad, a new Torah, with which you will open blind eyes, deaf ears and sealed hearts." Checked: al-Dārimī, *Musnad*, vol. 4, p. 2095 (Ḥusayn Asad ed.). Zawadi cites Ḥusayn Salīm Asad's verdict of good (no. 3370), which is not checked.
- Ibn ʿAbbās asked Kaʿb how he finds the Prophet's description in the Torah, and Kaʿb answered that he is Muhammad ibn ʿAbdillāh, born in Mecca, migrating to Ṭābah, with his kingdom in Syria. Checked: Ibn Rajab, *Faḍāʾil al-Shām*, p. 191, and Ibn ʿAsākir, *Tārīkh Dimashq*, vol. 1, p. 185.
- Kaʿb: "I find in the Torah that the congregational prayer is multiplied by the number of men." Partly checked: Shamela returns Ibn Rajab, *Fatḥ al-Bārī*, vol. 6, p. 19, but the snippet I saw did not show the sentence. Zawadi's reference is "Fatḥ al-Bārī, no. or page 4", and he calls it sound, which is not checked.
- Kaʿb and the hour on Friday: the Prophet's words on Friday and the hour of answered prayer, and Kaʿb read the Torah and said the Prophet was right. The exchange with Kaʿb is in Mālik, *al-Muwaṭṭaʾ*, vol. 2, p. 150 (al-Aʿẓamī ed.). Checked; Zawadi cites *Sunan Abī Dāwūd* 1041 and al-Albānī's grading at 1046. The report shows that Kaʿb found agreement in what he read. It does not say what the Torah of his day lacked.
- Abū l-ʿĀliya, as quoted by al-Ṭabarī on Q 2:41: it is written in the first book of the Jews, "O son of Adam, teach for free as you were taught for free." Partly checked: the same sentence is in *al-Kifāya fī al-tafsīr bi-l-maʾthūr*, vol. 2, p. 218, and I did not open al-Ṭabarī's page.
- Muqātil ibn Sulaymān, on Q 2:79: the Jewish leaders in Medina erased the description of Muhammad from the Torah and wrote something other than it. Checked: *Tafsīr Muqātil*, vol. 1, p. 118; also *al-Mawsūʿa fī al-tafsīr al-maʾthūr*, vol. 2, p. 499.
- The caliph al-Mahdī and the patriarch Timothy: Zawadi reports that al-Mahdī told Timothy the Bible had contained prophecies of Muhammad which the People of the Book removed, citing Timothy, p. 33 ff. Not checked. The source is a Christian account of the exchange.
- al-Qurṭubī, on Q 15:9, tells how a Jew who bought three copies of each of the Torah, the Gospel and the Qur'an, altered them all, sold the first two without complaint and found the Qur'an rejected, became a Muslim before the caliph al-Maʾmūn. Partly checked: the story is in Sibṭ Ibn al-Jawzī, *Mirʾāt al-zamān*, vol. 14, p. 185, and al-Suyūṭī, *al-Khaṣāʾiṣ al-kubrā*, vol. 2, p. 316. I did not open the passage in al-Qurṭubī.
- Sahl, a freedman of ʿUtba who was a Christian before he became a Muslim, said it is written in the Gospel that the Prophet is neither short nor tall, has a seal between his shoulders and is called Aḥmad. Zawadi cites Ibn Saʿd with a good chain and a verdict of good from al-Albānī. Not checked.

## The scholars {#scholars}

Zawadi names the following authors in a rough order of their death. Beside each is what he cites and where.

- al-Jāḥiẓ (d. 255/869), *al-Radd ʿalā al-Naṣārā*: the Christians took their religion from four men who were not protected from error, and the differences between their accounts show it. Checked: *al-Mukhtār fī al-radd ʿalā al-Naṣārā*, p. 71, and *Rasāʾil al-Jāḥiẓ*, vol. 3, p. 328. Zawadi uses David Thomas's translation in *The Bible in Arab Christianity*, p. 179.
- al-Qāḍī Ismāʿīl ibn Isḥāq (d. 282/896), asked why the Torah could be substituted and the Qur'an could not, answered that God left the Torah's keeping to its people (Q 5:44) and kept the Qur'an Himself (Q 15:9). Partly checked: the exchange is in al-Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ, *Tartīb al-madārik*, vol. 4, p. 283, from Abū ʿAmr al-Dānī, whom Zawadi names as his source in *Ṭabaqāt al-qurrāʾ*.
- al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923), on Q 2:79: those who distorted God's book wrote a book on the strength of their own interpretations, in opposition to what God revealed to Mūsā, and sold it. Checked: *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*, vol. 2, p. 165 (al-Turkī ed.).
- al-Ṭabarī, on Q 5:13: they distort the speech of their Lord, substitute it and write with their hands something other than what God sent down. Not checked. I did not find Zawadi's Arabic in al-Turkī's edition, and it may come from another edition.
- al-Ṭabarī, as quoted by Ibn Kathīr: the Qur'an is trustworthy over the earlier books, so what agrees with it is true and what disagrees with it is false. Zawadi gives it as a reading of Q 2:41 as well. Not checked here. The site cites the same idea from al-Ṭabarī's commentary on Q 5:48 in the [taḥrīf scholarship article](/what-classical-muslim-scholars-said-about-tahrif/).
- al-Zajjāj (d. 311/923), quoted by al-Samarqandī on Q 2:79: the Jewish leaders erased the description of Muhammad and wrote something else. Partly checked: *Baḥr al-ʿulūm*, vol. 1, p. 67 names al-Zajjāj beside it. al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-nuzūl*, p. 115, tells of the Jewish leaders who wrote what God had committed to them.
- al-Ṭaḥāwī (d. 321/933), *Sharḥ mushkil al-āthār*: asked how one can accept the Prophet's turning to a Torah whose people God said had written into it what was not from it. Partly checked: vol. 15, p. 94 discusses the Prophet's stoning of the Jewish couple.
- al-Māturīdī (d. 333/944), *Taʾwīlāt ahl al-sunna*, on the words "because of what their hands have sent forth": the saying "we are the children of God and His beloved" was not in the Gospel, and the Jews' "none will enter Paradise except Jews" was not in the Torah. They changed and substituted. Checked: vol. 10, p. 10.
- al-Muṭahhar ibn Ṭāhir al-Maqdisī (d. c. 355/966): Adang reports that he held the Torah altered from the beginning, even by the seventy elders at Sinai, with the text burned under Nebuchadnezzar, and that the Hebrew, Greek and Samaritan texts differ. Camilla Adang, *Muslim Writers on Judaism and the Hebrew Bible* (Brill, 1996), pp. 233–234. Not checked.
- Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ (d. 370/981), *Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*: their *taḥrīf* takes two forms, wrong interpretation, and change and substitution. Checked: vol. 2, p. 498 (al-ʿIlmiyya ed.).
- al-Ḥasan ibn Ayyūb (d. 378/988): "We have no doubt that the People of the Book changed some of their words." Zawadi cites Ibn Taymiyyah, *al-Jawāb al-ṣaḥīḥ*, vol. 2, p. 342. Not checked.
- Abū l-Ḥasan al-ʿĀmirī (d. 381/992), *al-Iʿlām bi-manāqib al-Islām*, p. 208: the four Gospels were written by Christ's students and hold reports of his life from birth to his last days. Not checked. The sentence, as Zawadi gives it, describes who wrote them. Zawadi reads it as an admission that they are not revealed.
- al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār (d. 415/1025), *Tathbīt dalāʾil al-nubuwwa*: the Christians do not know who the four evangelists were, and Luke only asserts that he saw Christ's companions. Partly checked: vol. 1, p. 155 argues on the Gospels, but I did not match Zawadi's wording.
- al-Bīrūnī (d. 440/1048), *al-Āthār al-bāqiya*, pp. 13 ff. and 32 f.: Zawadi says he held that Christians had misunderstood the scriptures and that the genuine text had been materially corrupted. Not checked.
- Ibn Ḥazm (d. 456/1064), *al-Fiṣal fī al-milal*. Four passages: that the Christians themselves do not claim the Gospels were revealed, and name four writers and dates (vol. 2, p. 2); that only 120 men believed in the Messiah in his lifetime, and the revealed Gospel was lost apart from a few verses (vol. 2, pp. 4–5); and that each of the Jews and the Christians has truth and falsehood in its books (vol. 2, p. 11). Partly checked: I found the sentence «a|وأما النصارى فقد كفونا هذه المؤونة» in vol. 2, p. 2. The other passages are not checked.
- al-Juwaynī (d. 478/1085): Zawadi says that *Shifāʾ al-ghalīl fī bayān mā waqaʿa fī al-Tawrāt wa-l-Injīl min al-tabdīl* points to contradictions in the Gospels and doubts the raised saints of Matthew 27:52 because no other Gospel reports them. Not checked. I could not confirm the attribution of the book.
- al-Baghawī (d. 516/1122), *Maʿālim al-tanzīl*, on Q 2:79: the Jews changed the description of the Prophet and wrote something besides it. Not checked.
- al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538/1144), *al-Kashshāf*, vol. 2, p. 575: the Qur'an claims to confirm, guard and test the truth held in the Torah and the Gospel. Not checked. Zawadi reads "the truth contained in" as a sign that falsehood is also contained.
- Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī (d. 606/1210), on Q 4:46: "they distort words from their places" means false interpretation, and there is no proof that they took the wording out of the book; Q 5:41 shows both together, since "after their places" points to removal of the wording. Checked: *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, vol. 10, p. 93.

## What the list shows {#shows}

Most entries fall into three kinds.

Some report that certain Jews of Medina changed or hid particular passages: the stoning verse, the description of the Prophet, the words of a greeting. Those reports name a group, a place and a subject.

Some quote scholars from the tenth to the fourteenth century who say that wording was changed, often alongside meaning. al-Jaṣṣāṣ, al-Rāzī on Q 5:41 and Ibn Taymiyyah as Ibn Kathīr reports him all hold both.

The rest rest on one inference: a detail that a Companion or an early convert reports from the Torah or the Gospel is not found in the Bible today, so it must have been removed. The source gives the detail, and the removal is Zawadi's reading.

The site's position is the same as it was before this list went in. The revelation was given by God, the later record was changed in places by some of those who held it, and the Qur'an is its guardian and judge.
