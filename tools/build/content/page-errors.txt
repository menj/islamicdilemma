title: Errors in the case for the dilemma
excerpt: Misdated history, mismatched verses, half-quoted passages and claims that contradict each other. A list of the factual errors in the argument's best-known presentation, each with the text that corrects it.
order: 11

!lead An argument is only as strong as its facts. The best-known presentation of the Islamic Dilemma makes its case across a series of pages, and in checking them verse by verse we found errors of several kinds: wrong dates, English translations printed under the Arabic of a different verse, verses quoted without their second half, headings that say the opposite of the section beneath them, and claims on one page that another page contradicts. This page lists them in one place, with the text that corrects each one, so that readers can check them for themselves.

Whatever their cause, the effect is the same: a reader who trusts the presentation comes away believing the Qur'an says things it does not say.

## How we checked {#method}

Every Qur'anic verse below was compared with the standard Arabic text of the Qur'an and its verse numbering. Every hadith is given with its number in the standard editions, so that it can be looked up. Bible quotations are from the ESV unless noted. We quote the presentation's own words exactly, including its spelling, so that nothing is attributed to it that it does not say.

## 1. History and dates {#dates}

**"A book in the hands of Christians in 700."** The presentation says twice that the Qur'an describes the Gospel as being in Christian hands "in 700", and asks: "If the Gospel was in their hands in 700 AD and it was already corrupt why did they not say that?"

The Prophet Muhammad died in 632, and the revelation of the Qur'an ended with his death. Nothing in the Qur'an can describe the year 700. The date is wrong by most of a century, and it is repeated. Nor do Muslims claim the Injil is a separate book that went missing; see [Injil: where is it?](/injil-where-is-it/)


## 2. Verses printed under the wrong Arabic {#mismatches}

The presentation offers three contrasts between the Bible and the Qur'an. In two of them, the English translation and the Arabic text belong to different verses.

**The "Mediator" contrast.** The section quotes 1 Timothy 2:5, "one mediator between God and mankind", and sets against it the English of Q 4:171: "do not say, 'Three'; desist". Beneath that English it prints an Arabic text that is not Q 4:171 at all. It is the Throne Verse, Q 2:255: "God, there is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer ... Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission?" So the Arabic printed under the heading about mediation is a verse that *affirms* intercession, by God's permission. Whatever contrast the section intended, the verse it shows in Arabic does not make it.

**The "Tawhid versus Trinity" contrast.** The next section quotes 1 Corinthians 8:6 and sets against it the English of Q 3:185: "Every soul will taste death, and you will only be given your [full] compensation on the Day of Resurrection." That verse is about death and judgment, not about the Trinity. The Arabic beneath it is the opening of Q 4:171, "do not say: Three". The English and the Arabic come from two different surahs, and the English says nothing on the subject of the heading.

**Q 3:185 quoted twice.** The same English of Q 3:185 appears again in the third contrast, on eternal life, so one verse about death serves two different arguments.

These three errors are examined in detail in [Three contrasts, three misquotations](/three-contrasts-three-misquotations/).

## 3. A verse read as the opposite of what it says {#3-185}

The third contrast presents Q 3:185 as if the Qur'an offered only death and uncertainty, against the Christian hope of eternal life. But the verse goes on: "So he who is drawn away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has attained his desire." It promises Paradise. And the Bible says the same thing about death that 3:185 says:

> "And just as it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment." -- Hebrews 9:27 · ESV

Both books teach that every person dies and is judged, and that the faithful receive eternal life.

## 4. Headings that contradict their sections {#headings}

**"The Injil is not the Christian Gospel."** One section carries this heading, and then argues for the opposite: that the Injil the Qur'an mentions *is* the Gospel Christians hold.

**The presentation's own definition.** A separate page on the word *Injil* states that "in the Qur'an, 'Injil' specifically refers to the revelation given to Jesus by God", and that "in Islamic theology, it denotes the holy book revealed to Jesus." That is the Muslim position, stated accurately. It is also incompatible with the main argument, which needs the Qur'an's Injil to be the four Gospels written about Jesus by others. The presentation cannot hold both.

**"Muslims Are Commanded to Judge by the Gospel."** This heading sits over Q 5:47. But the verse does not address Muslims. It says: "Let the people of the Gospel judge by what God has revealed therein." The presentation's own summary elsewhere says, correctly, that in 5:47 "Christians are commanded". The very next verse tells the Prophet, and through him the Muslims, to judge by the Qur'an {Q 5:48}. See [Judge by it, uphold it](/judge-by-it-uphold-it/).

## 5. Verses quoted in part {#partial}

**Q 5:48.** The presentation says that in this passage "the Qur'an say the Injil is a physical book in the hands of Christians". The verse says nothing about a physical book. It tells the Prophet that the Qur'an was sent down "confirming what came before it of the Book and as a guardian over it", and commands him: "judge between them by what God has revealed, and do not follow their desires away from the truth that has come to you." The presentation quotes the last phrase, but the "truth that has come to you" is the Qur'an, and the verse places it over the earlier scripture. Its key word, *muhaymin*, guardian, is explained in [What does Qur'an 5:48 actually confirm?](/what-does-quran-5-48-confirm/)

**Q 5:68.** The presentation quotes the first half: "you have nothing to stand on until you uphold the Torah, the Gospel and what has been sent down to you from your Lord." It stops there. The second half says: "And what has been sent down to you from your Lord will surely increase many of them in transgression and disbelief." The phrase "what has been sent down to you" is the Qur'an, and the verse predicts that many of the People of the Book will reject it. Read whole, 5:68 calls them to the Qur'an.

**Q 18:27.** The presentation lists this verse as "No one can change Allah's words", and applies it to the Bible. The verse reads:

::ayah Q 18:27 | وَٱتْلُ مَآ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْكَ مِن كِتَابِ رَبِّكَ ۖ لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَـٰتِهِۦ وَلَن تَجِدَ مِن دُونِهِۦ مُلْتَحَدًۭا | Recite what has been revealed to you of the Book of your Lord. None can change His words, and you will find no refuge besides Him.

It is about what was revealed to the Prophet, the Qur'an. It does not mention the Torah or the Gospel. See ["None can change His words"](/none-can-change-his-words/).

## 6. Claims that contradict each other {#contradictions}

**Q 10:94 and the Prophet's "doubts".** The presentation says: "When Muhammad had doubts about his revelations, Allah commanded him to seek confirmation from the people of the Bible". In the next sentence it says: "It was not that he doubted but if he doubted ..." The two sentences cannot both be true. The second is correct: the verse is a conditional, "if you are in doubt", a common form of address whose lesson is for the listeners. Al-Ṭabarī reports, through Qatādah, that the Prophet said when it was revealed: "I do not doubt, and I do not ask."[1] See [Ask those who read the Scripture](/ask-those-who-read-scripture/).

**"Never said that themselves."** The presentation says that "Allah, the Angel Gabriel, and Muhammad never said" the scripture of the People of the Book had been corrupted. The Qur'an says it directly, more than once:

::ayah Q 2:79 | فَوَيْلٌۭ لِّلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَـٰذَا مِنْ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ | So woe to those who write the book with their own hands and then say, "This is from God."

It speaks of a party who heard the word of God and "distorted it after they had understood it" {Q 2:75}, of some who "twist their tongues with the Book" so that it is taken as scripture when it is not {Q 3:78}, and of those who "distort words from their places and have forgotten a portion" of what they were reminded of {Q 5:13}. One may argue about what these verses cover. One cannot say the Qur'an never says it. See [How the Qur'an describes alteration](/how-quran-describes-alteration/).

**"Every reference to the Gospel affirms it."** The presentation lists the Qur'an's references to the Gospel and says each one affirms it. Its list includes Q 7:157, which says the People of the Book find the Prophet Muhammad "written with them in the Torah and the Gospel", and Q 57:27, which distinguishes the Gospel God gave Jesus from the monasticism his followers "invented", and adds that "many of them are defiantly disobedient". The first makes a claim about the Gospel's content that the presentation rejects; the second criticises what was added to it. Neither is discussed. See [Every verse on the Gospel, read in full](/every-verse-on-gospel/).

**"Guidance would mislead."** One page asks how the Torah and Gospel could have been corrupted if God gave them as guidance, and says: "This creates a tension with the belief that Allah's guidance would mislead people." No Muslim belief says God's guidance misleads. The sentence appears to mean the reverse of what it says. The Qur'an's answer to the underlying question is that God guaranteed the Qur'an's preservation Himself {Q 15:9}, while the earlier books were entrusted to their communities {Q 5:44}.

## 7. What Islam teaches about sin and forgiveness {#sin}

**"Islam says you can get your own sins forgiven."** The presentation calls this a lie, but the description is wrong to begin with. Islam teaches that forgiveness comes from God alone: "and who forgives sins except God?" {Q 3:135}. A Muslim asks God for forgiveness; no one forgives himself.

**"Good works balances small sins."** The Qur'an does say that "good deeds remove bad deeds" {Q 11:114}. But it does not teach that a person earns Paradise by outweighing his sins. The Prophet said: "None of you will enter Paradise by his deeds alone." When asked, "Not even you?", he said: "Not even me, unless God covers me with His mercy."[2] Salvation in Islam rests on God's mercy, received through faith, repentance and righteous action. See [Sin and repentance in Islam](/sin-and-repentance/).

**"One tiny lie has the same impact as Murder to God."** This is offered as Christian teaching, but the New Testament grades sin. Jesus tells Pilate that "he who delivered me over to you has the greater sin" {John 19:11}, and says that "it will be more bearable on the day of judgment for Tyre and Sidon" than for the towns that rejected him {Matt 11:22}. A greater sin and a more bearable judgment only make sense if sins differ in weight.

## 8. Claims about the Bible {#bible}

**"The Bible agrees with itself 1,000 times."** Christian scholars themselves discuss passages that are hard to reconcile. Mark says Jesus was crucified at "the third hour" {Mark 15:25}; John places him before Pilate at "about the sixth hour" {John 19:14}. Matthew says Judas hanged himself (Matthew 27:5); Acts says he fell headlong and burst open (Acts 1:18). Christians offer harmonisations, but the claim as stated is not one Christian scholarship would make.

**1 Corinthians 8:6 as a proof of the Trinity.** The verse is quoted to show that the Bible teaches the Trinity. It says: "for us there is one God, the Father ... and one Lord, Jesus Christ." It names the one God as the Father, distinguishes Jesus from Him as Lord, and does not mention the Holy Spirit. Whatever else one makes of it, it is not a statement of three persons in one God.

**Assurance and falling away.** The presentation says that in Christ "the verdict is final" and that no sin or failure "can bring us under judgment again". Elsewhere it quotes Hebrews 6:4–6: "It is impossible for those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift... and who have fallen away, to be brought back to repentance". The passage it quotes describes believers who fall away beyond repentance, which is difficult to square with its own claim that no failure can bring a believer under judgment.

## 9. Descriptions of Islam {#descriptions}

Several devotional pages contrast Christianity with "religions of works or distant deities", "distant or impersonal gods", and "systems where access to God depends on intermediaries or rituals". If these are meant to include Islam, they describe it wrongly. The Qur'an says God is "closer to him than his jugular vein" {Q 50:16}, and:

> "When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls on Me." -- Q 2:186

Islam has no priesthood and no intermediary between a believer and God. The contrast works only against a religion Islam is not. See [Is the God of Islam distant?](/is-god-of-islam-distant/)

## What the errors show {#conclusion}

Taken one by one, some of these errors are small. Taken together, they form a pattern. The verses the argument relies on most, 5:47, 5:48, 5:68, 10:94 and 18:27, are each quoted in part, applied to the wrong subject, or given a heading they do not support. The verses that cut against the argument, 2:75, 2:79, 5:13, 7:157 and 57:27, are left out or listed without comment. And on two points, the meaning of *Injil* and whether the Prophet doubted, the presentation corrects itself in the very sentences that make the error.

Muslims should hold themselves to the same standard. Every verse we quote should be checked in Arabic, in context, before it is used; every hadith should carry its reference. If you find an error on this site, [tell us](/about/) and we will correct it.

!callout **In short.** Read with the Arabic and the surrounding verses, the case for the dilemma rests on misdated history, mismatched translations and half-quoted passages. Corrected, the verses say what Muslims have always said: the Qur'an confirms the revelation God gave Jesus and stands guardian over what was made of it.
