!lead Four short posts on Sam Shamoun's blog quote al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Qurṭubī on Q 5:48 and Q 66:12. Each puts a few words in capitals and stops. This article reads the same excerpts, in the same English, down to the lines the capitals skip.

::ayah Q 5:48 | وَأَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ بِٱلْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًۭا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ وَمُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ ۖ | To thee We sent the Scripture in truth, confirming the scripture that came before it, and guarding it in safety…

## What the posts claim {#claim}

The posts are titled "Al-Tabari on Q. 5:48", "Ar-Razi on Q. 5:48 & Q. 66:12", "Al-Baidawi on Q. 3:45-50, 5:46-48 & Q. 66:12" and "Al-Qurtubi on Q. 66:12 & Mary as Muhammad's Wife".[1] They share one argument.

The Qur'an calls itself *muhaymin*, guardian, over the earlier scripture. Mary "testified to the truth" of God's books {Q 66:12}. So, the posts conclude, the Torah and the Gospel were intact when the Qur'an came, they are the Bible Christians read today, and the Qur'an stands refuted by the books it vouched for.

That is the first claim of the Islamic Dilemma, argued from Muslim sources. It is a fair method. A Muslim who cites al-Ṭabarī should accept what al-Ṭabarī says.

So the test here is simple. Each excerpt below comes from Shamoun's own translation, on his own page.

## Al-Ṭabarī: a witness that judges {#tabari}

Al-Ṭabarī defines the root of *muhaymin* as "preservation and watchfulness". Then he collects the early reports. Shamoun prints them in full, and two of them settle the question.

The first comes from Ibn ʿAbbās through al-ʿAwfī's chain: «a|أمينا عليه، يحكم على ما كان قبله من الكتب». In Shamoun's translation: "'And as a guardian over it' means trustworthy over it, judging the scriptures that came before it."[2]

The second comes from Ibn Jurayj: «a|القرآن أمين على الكتب فيما إذ أخبرنا أهل الكتاب في كتابهم بأمر إن كان في القرآن فصدقوا، وإلا فكذبوا». Shamoun renders it: "The Qur'an is entrusted with the Scriptures: when it informs us that the People of the Book have something in their Scripture, if it is also in the Qur'an, they have spoken truthfully; otherwise, they have lied."

Both lines sit in the post Shamoun titled as proof of the Bible's integrity. Both make the Qur'an the test of what the People of the Book say about their scripture.

A test of that kind has work to do only if some claims fail it. If every line of the earlier books were secure, Ibn Jurayj would have no "otherwise" to write.

Ibn Kathīr quotes the same Ibn Jurayj report in sharper words, as the article on [what Q 5:48 confirms](/what-does-quran-5-48-confirm/#classical) shows. The earliest commentators read *muhaymin* as a judge over the earlier books, and al-Ṭabarī records them doing so.

## Al-Rāzī: one book set apart from the rest {#razi}

Al-Rāzī gives two derivations of *muhaymin*, watcher and trustee. Then he explains why the Qur'an has the role. Shamoun's translation reads: "The Quran is a guardian over the Scriptures because it is a Book that will never be abrogated, and it cannot be subject to alteration or distortion".

The Arabic is plain: «a|لأنه الكتاب الذي لا يصير منسوخا ألبتة، ولا يتطرق إليه التبديل والتحريف». Al-Rāzī supports it with Q 15:9, where God promises to guard the Qur'an.

::ayah Q 15:9 | إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا ٱلذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَحَـٰفِظُونَ | We have, without doubt, sent down the Message; and We will assuredly guard it (from corruption).

His reason is a contrast. The Qur'an is guardian because it alone cannot be abrogated or altered. A reason of that shape assumes the other books lack the protection.

Then comes the sentence Shamoun puts in capitals: the Qur'an's testimony that the Torah, the Gospel and the Psalms are true "remain[s] so forever", and that "makes the truth of these scriptures known forever".

Read the grammar. The permanence belongs to the Qur'an's testimony, and the testimony lasts because the Qur'an is guarded. Al-Rāzī says nothing here about the copies of the Torah in seventh-century Medina.

The same post reports al-Rāzī's gloss on the first words of the verse: "confirming what came before it" means "every book sent down from heaven other than the Quran". That list includes the scrolls of Abraham {Q 87:18–19}, which no community has held for millennia.

So al-Rāzī's confirmation covers books that no longer exist anywhere. It confirms revelations, including revelations with no surviving copy, and the article on [the scrolls of Abraham and Moses](/scrolls-of-abraham-and-moses-forgiveness-without-blood/) works through what follows from that.

The post also stops after al-Rāzī's second point. His third reports al-Zamakhsharī's reading *muhayman ʿalayhi*, in which the Qur'an is the thing God guards from distortion.[3]

Then al-Rāzī reaches the command that follows, "so judge between them". His gloss is six words: «a|فاحكم بين اليهود بالقرآن», "judge between the Jews by the Qur'an". The commentator Shamoun cites for the Bible's authority sends the judgment to the Qur'an.

## Al-Bayḍāwī: guarded from change, by what? {#baydawi}

Al-Bayḍāwī glosses the word in one line: «a|رقيبا على سائر الكتب يحفظه عن التغيير ويشهد له بالصحة والثبات». Shamoun's translation: "a watcher over all the other scriptures, preserving it from alteration and testifying to its truth and enduring validity".

The subject of "preserving" is the Qur'an. The Qur'an preserves the earlier revelation from alteration by carrying its true content and testifying to it. On this reading, the earlier message survives intact inside the Qur'an.

That is the opposite of the use Shamoun makes of the line. If the Torah scrolls had kept themselves free of change, they would need no watcher to preserve them.

### "Which they still possess" {#possess}

Shamoun's introduction says al-Bayḍāwī has Christians "commanded to judge by Christ's revelations, which they still possess". The last four words belong to Shamoun. Al-Bayḍāwī's excerpt never says them.

What al-Bayḍāwī does say on Q 5:47 is that the verse records the Gospel's own law and shows that "Judaism was abrogated with the mission of Jesus". He explains one reading of the verse as an omitted verb with a purpose clause, «a|وآتيناه ليحكم», which Shamoun translates "We gave it to him so that he might judge". That describes the purpose of the Gospel when it was given.

The site's article on [Q 5:47, 5:66 and 5:68](/judge-by-it-uphold-it/#two-readings) sets out both readings of that verse.

Then al-Bayḍāwī turns to Q 5:48 and its command: "So judge between them by what God has revealed to you." If one revelation could take over from the Torah, the next one can take over from the Gospel. Al-Bayḍāwī's own principle carries the argument one verse further than Shamoun takes it.

### Al-Bayḍāwī on Jesus {#jesus}

The same post quotes al-Bayḍāwī on Q 3:46, that Jesus speaks in the cradle and in maturity. Al-Bayḍāwī says those changing states «a|إرشادا إلى أنه بمعزل عن الألوهية», "are mentioned to show that he is far removed from divinity".

Shamoun prints that sentence without comment. A commentator trusted for one gloss deserves the same hearing on the next.

## What three commentators say about Mary's "books" {#mary}

> And Mary the daughter of 'Imran, who guarded her chastity; and We breathed into (her body) of Our spirit; and she testified to the truth of the words of her Lord and of His Revelations, and was one of the devout (servants). -- Q 66:12

The posts read "His books" as proof that the Bible stood intact in Mary's day. The commentators Shamoun quotes explain the phrase differently.

Al-Rāzī says God's words are "the revealed scrolls given to Idris and others", and His books are "the four scriptures". He adds that the phrase may mean everything God spoke to His angels and inscribed "in the Preserved Tablet".

Al-Bayḍāwī gives "what was written in the Preserved Tablet, or the revealed scriptures as a whole". Al-Qurṭubī takes "the Book" as a category that covers every book God revealed.[4]

Each of the three describes faith in revelation, the same faith the Qur'an asks of every believer, who "believeth in God, His angels, His books, and His messengers" {Q 2:285}.

There is also a plain matter of dates. Mary is praised for her faith in her own lifetime. The four Gospels were written decades after the ministry of Jesus, so the books she affirmed cannot have included them.

## "His Book" or "His books" {#readings}

The al-Qurṭubī post goes one step further. It says the variant readings of Q 66:12 show "that the Quran has not been preserved from textual variations and corruption".

Al-Qurṭubī lists the readings himself: «a|وقرأ أبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم وكتبه جمعا … والباقون بكتابه على التوحيد». Abū ʿAmr, and Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim, read «q|وَكُتُبِهِۦ», "and His books". The other readers read "and His Book", singular. Ḥafṣ is the reading most Muslims recite today.

Both are canonical readings, and the Qur'an's early spelling writes the two in the same consonants. One written text, voiced in two ways that were both taught from the Prophet, gives the singular and the plural.

The other wordings al-Qurṭubī mentions, Ubayy's "into the opening of her garment" and al-Ḥasan's "the word of her Lord", sit outside the canonical readings. Commentators record such wordings as explanation, and no Muslim recites them as Qur'an.

The site's article on [the *qirāʾāt* and the preservation of the Qur'an](/qiraat-and-preservation-of-quran/) sets out how the readings were documented. Al-Qurṭubī reports the readings as readings. "Corruption" is Shamoun's word for them.

## What the four excerpts show {#show}

Shamoun chose these commentators. In his own translations they say four things.

- Al-Ṭabarī, from Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Jurayj: the earlier books are judged by the Qur'an, and what disagrees with it is false.
- Al-Rāzī: the Qur'an is guardian because it alone is safe from alteration.
- Al-Bayḍāwī: the Qur'an keeps the earlier revelation from alteration by carrying it, and its law supersedes the Gospel's as the Gospel's superseded the Torah's.
- Al-Qurṭubī: the readings of Q 66:12 are canonical variants of one written text.

That is the classical position the Islamic Dilemma leaves out: the earlier books came from God, they survive in part, and the Qur'an is the measure. The site's article on [two standards](/two-standards-method-behind-dilemma/#selective) found the same pattern in Shamoun's older work on *muhaymin*.

Al-Fārūqī's first rule of knowledge is to reject whatever does not correspond with reality. The excerpts, read to their last line, do not correspond with the conclusion drawn from them.
