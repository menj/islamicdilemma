!lead Islam's message is short enough to fit on one page. It is, the Qur'an says, the message every prophet brought: worship God alone, and live as someone who will meet Him. This page sets it out as simply as we can, for anyone who wants to know what Muslims believe and why.

## One God {#one-god}

::ayah Q 112:1–4 | قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ | Say: He is Allah, the One and Only; Allah, the Eternal, Absolute; He begetteth not, nor is He begotten; And there is none like unto Him.

This short surah, recited daily by Muslims around the world, is the heart of Islam. God is one in His being, in His names and attributes, and in His right to be worshipped.

He has no partner, no son and no equal. He was not born and does not die.

Nothing in creation shares His divinity, however honoured it is. That includes angels, prophets and saints. This is *tawḥīd*, the oneness of God, and it is the first thing the Qur'an asks of every person.

*Tawḥīd* is a doctrine, and it is also a kind of freedom.

If God alone is God, then no person, no ruler, no fear and no desire has the right to rule a human being's heart. A Muslim bows to God and to nothing else.

## Why we exist {#why-we-exist}

The Qur'an gives a direct answer to the question of why human beings exist: "I did not create jinn and humankind except to worship Me" {Q 51:56}. Worship, in the Qur'an's sense, is far wider than ritual.

It includes prayer and fasting, but it also includes honesty in trade, kindness to parents, care for orphans, justice to enemies, gratitude for food and patience in illness. Every action done for God's sake, in the way He approves, is worship.

That gives every life a purpose that does not depend on wealth, status or success. A poor farmer who prays, works honestly and treats people justly has fulfilled the purpose of his existence as fully as anyone who has ever lived.

## Near, loving and merciful {#near}

::ayah Q 2:186 | وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِى عَنِّى فَإِنِّى قَرِيبٌ ۖ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ ۖ | When My servants ask thee concerning Me, I am indeed close (to them): I listen to the prayer of every suppliant when he calleth on Me…

Islam has no priesthood standing between a person and God, and no ritual or intermediary is needed for a prayer to be heard. God is "nearer to him than his jugular vein" {Q 50:16}.

Among His names are *al-Raḥmān* and *al-Raḥīm*, the Most Merciful and the Especially Merciful, which open every surah of the Qur'an but one, and *al-Wadūd*, the Loving {Q 85:14}. "My mercy encompasses all things" {Q 7:156}.

People who have heard that the God of Islam is distant or harsh are often surprised by how much of the Qur'an is about mercy. [Is the God of Islam distant?](/is-god-of-islam-distant/) sets out what the Qur'an and the Prophet taught about God's nearness and love.

## Accountable, each for our own {#accountable}

Every person will answer to God for what they did. "Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it" {Q 99:7–8}.

But no one is born guilty of another's sin, and no one carries another's burden: "no bearer of burdens bears the burden of another" {Q 6:164}. God "does not burden a soul beyond its capacity" {Q 2:286}.

What God asks is faith, prayer, honesty, charity and turning back to Him when we fall short. When we sin, as everyone does, the door of repentance is open until death, and God has promised to accept those who turn to Him sincerely. [Sin and repentance in Islam](/sin-and-repentance/) explains how forgiveness works, and why Islam does not teach that people forgive their own sins.

## The prophets, one message {#prophets}

The Qur'an teaches that God has never left humanity without guidance. He sent prophets to every people {Q 16:36}, and their message was the same: "We sent no messenger before you without revealing to him: there is no god but Me, so worship Me" {Q 21:25}.

Muslims believe in Adam, Noah, Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, David, Solomon, Jonah, John the Baptist and Jesus, among others, and honour all of them. To deny any of them would be to leave Islam.

Muslims also believe in the books God gave the prophets: the scrolls of Abraham, the Torah of Moses, the Psalms of David and the Gospel of Jesus. The Qur'an confirms what remains true in them and corrects what was lost or changed in human hands {Q 5:48}. That is the subject of most of this site, beginning with [the Muslim response](/muslim-response/).

## Jesus in Islam {#jesus}

Many Christians are surprised to learn how highly the Qur'an honours Jesus. It calls him the Messiah, *al-Masīḥ*.

It affirms that he was born of the virgin Mary by God's command, without a father {Q 3:47}. It calls him "a word from God" and "a spirit from Him" {Q 4:171}.

It records that he spoke as an infant in his mother's defence, healed the blind and the leper, and raised the dead, all by God's permission {Q 5:110}. Muslims believe he will return before the end of time.

The Qur'an also records what Jesus said about himself, as a newborn in the cradle: "I am the servant of God. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet" {Q 19:30}.

And it records his message to his people: "worship God, my Lord and your Lord" {Q 5:72}. The Gospels preserve sayings in the same spirit: "the Lord our God, the Lord is one" {Mark 12:29}; "I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God" {John 20:17}.

Muslims follow Jesus in that message. [An invitation to Christians](/invitation-to-christians/) explains the Muslim view of Jesus more fully.

## Muhammad and the Qur'an {#muhammad}

Muhammad, peace be upon him, was born in Mecca around 570 and received the first revelation of the Qur'an in 610, at the age of forty. Over twenty-three years, the Qur'an was revealed to him, recited by him, memorised by his companions and written down.

He died in 632. Muslims believe he is the last of the prophets, sent to all humanity, and that the Qur'an is God's final revelation, which God promised to guard Himself: "It is We who sent down the Reminder, and it is We who will guard it" {Q 15:9}.

The Qur'an has been recited in the same Arabic words, from memory and in writing, for fourteen centuries. Millions of Muslims today know it entirely by heart. That continuous transmission is, for Muslims, a sign of the promise in 15:9 being kept.

## Living as a Muslim {#living}

Islam is built on five practices, often called its pillars: bearing witness that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His messenger; praying five times a day; giving a portion of one's wealth to those in need; fasting the month of Ramadan; and making the pilgrimage to Mecca once in a lifetime, for those who are able. Around these is a whole way of life: honesty, modesty, kindness to neighbours, care for parents, justice, and the remembrance of God in ordinary moments.

None of this is meant to be a burden for its own sake. The Qur'an says that God "intends ease for you and does not intend hardship" {Q 2:185}. The practices are ways of remembering God and drawing near to Him, day by day.

## Questions people often ask {#questions}

**Is Islam only for Arabs?** No. Most Muslims live outside the Arab world; the largest Muslim populations are in Indonesia, South Asia and Africa. The Qur'an says God made people "into nations and tribes so that you may know one another", and that the most honoured in His sight is the most righteous {Q 49:13}.

**Do Muslims worship Muhammad?** No. Muslims love and follow the Prophet, but they worship God alone.

Muhammad is described in the Qur'an as "a man like you" to whom revelation came {Q 18:110}. Praying to him, or to anyone besides God, would be *shirk*.

**What happens to people who never heard the message?** The Qur'an says God does not punish a people until He has sent them a messenger {Q 17:15}. God judges each person justly, with full knowledge of what they knew and could have known.

**Does Islam teach that Christians and Jews are going to Hell?** The Qur'an speaks of reward for those among the People of the Book who believe and do good {Q 3:199}, and it leaves the final judgment of every person to God, who knows what each person knew. What Muslims are commanded to do is to convey the message clearly and courteously, and to invite others to it. Judgment belongs to God alone.

## Becoming Muslim {#becoming}

Becoming Muslim is simple. It needs no priest, no ceremony and no permission from anyone. A person who believes bears witness, sincerely, in their own words or in these:

::shahada

That declaration, the *shahādah*, is the whole of the entry into Islam. Everything before it is forgiven {Q 8:38}.

Many new Muslims make it in front of others, at a mosque or among friends, because it is a joy to share. But it is valid between a person and God alone.

There is "no compulsion in religion" {Q 2:256}. If you are thinking about Islam, take your time.

Read the Qur'an for yourself in a good translation, from the beginning. Speak to Muslims you trust, and ask your hardest questions.

Pray to God, in whatever words you have, to guide you to the truth. Muslims make that prayer in every unit of every prayer: "Guide us to the straight path" {Q 1:6}.
